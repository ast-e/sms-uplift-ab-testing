SMS Uplift Experiment

Did a text message make customers buy more, and is it worth sending? X5 Retail Group tested it on 200,039 customers in 2019.

Analysis by Eugenio Astocondor of an experiment run by X5 Retail Group in 2019

Conclusion. The SMS increased purchases. The difference is statistically significant (p < 0.001) and holds when adjusted for past spending (+3.2 pp). Whether it pays off depends on the margin of an extra sale (see below).

Who reacts most?

The effect is larger for older customers and for recent cardholders. These are exploratory comparisons and should be confirmed in a follow-up test.

Is it worth sending?

The data has no cost or profit figures, so these are example numbers. At 5 cents per message, each extra sale must bring in at least €1.50 of profit for the campaign to pay for itself. For customers aged 65+ the bar is €0.85, and for those aged 16-24 it is €3.73.

Recommendation. Send the message where an extra sale is worth more than that bar, start with the groups that react most, and keep a small group unmessaged so the effect can keep being measured.

Data. X5 Retail Group, a Russian food retailer, published this anonymised experiment for its RetailHero uplift modelling competition in 2019. I did not run it. The data is not redistributed here.

Method. Two-proportion z-test, sample-ratio and balance checks, CUPED adjustment, segment analysis with multiple-testing correction. Full statistical analysis, limitations and a proposed design for the next test are in the technical repository.
